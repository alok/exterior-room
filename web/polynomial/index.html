Orientation convention

Right-handed display basis X = −b, Y = a+c, Z = c−a, with e_x × e_y = e_z; local camera forward is −e_z. ZX turns about +Y, YZ about +X, XY about +Z. The existing ordered camera factorization is C = R_ZX(θ) R_YZ(φ) R_XY(ψ), equivalent to Euler YXZ. The rightmost factor acts first on column vectors. After ZX, the YZ and XY plane labels use the successively rotated camera basis. Drag changes the first two factors. These factors do not commute.

Eye at (0,0,0), the zero polynomial: no unique root or projective point; radial projection is undefined. Each line of sight is one class of nonzero forms; k·(a,b,c) share a ray, and ±(a,b,c), with the same roots, lie opposite. Looking up, the upper nappe appears as the Klein disk.

With Move forms off, drag to look; W/A/S/D and Q/E move the eye. Move forms changes the lattice by a Möbius action from Origin.

What am I seeing?

Each triple is ax²+bx+c, drawn at (X,Y,Z) = (−b, a+c, c−a), so Δ = b²−4ac = X²+Z²−Y². Upper interior (Δ<0, a>0) is colored by |Δ|: one color per sheet Y²−X²−Z² = |Δ|, a level set preserved by SL₂(ℤ). Exterior (Δ>0) has real roots, the cone (Δ=0) a double root; the lower interior repeats the upper roots with opposite sign. A white ring marks the selection.

The whole lattice shows integer coefficient triples in a finite ball x²+y²+z²≤R², including both signs. Turn Whole coefficient lattice off to keep only a>0, Δ<0. The full transformed grid is not arbitrary integer (x,y,z).

The optional screen y=4 cuts the upper cone in a disk. A lower-half triple is sign-flipped first; a polynomial and its negative have the same roots. Radial projection multiplies every coordinate by 4/(a+c). Multiples, such as (1,−1,2) and (2,−2,4), lie at different depths but share one image and one quadratic root.

The normalized disk coordinates u=−b/(a+c), v=(c−a)/(a+c) map to the upper half-plane by u/(1−v) + i√(1−u²−v²)/(1−v), the quadratic root shown above.

Sources and scope

Separate Lean-backed adaptation of FareyVision’s coefficient-cone construction in Katherine Stange’s talk and Starscape / Lattice viewer. Our symmetric cone extent and origin-centered crop differ from the original viewer’s asymmetric framing. Interior color uses her fixed logarithmic 3–240 |Δ| range, so equal Δ has equal position in both viewers; nonprimitive multiples keep their own Δ. The default maps it onto Viridis (Smith, van der Walt and Firing; CC0, license) from 0.2 to 1, skipping the darkest fifth, which is too dark for single-pixel dots on black; its exterior, cone and lower colors are our choice, each at least 0.1 in OKLab from the ramp under normal, protan and deutan simulation. Stange anchors uses her six anchors with OKLab interpolation and the earlier gold, blue and gray categories; her viewer derives category colors from OKLCH lightness and chroma, and those exact values are not verified here. Lower-half coloring is an opt-in extension. Dots are flat, unlit discs with centers derived from Lean data and converted to floating-point renderer coordinates. Dot size follows the causes in her viewer: perspective and a fixed star radius (nonprimitive upper stars 0.72×, upper stars on a selected plane 1.35×), overview fog, and in Origin her eye-at-origin radius ∝ |Δ|^¼/√(a+c), so apparent size there is 1/√|Δ| times a Klein-position factor. Ours: radii above 2.5 px grow only as the cube root, capped at 9 px; below 1 px a dot keeps 1 px and dims; at the origin off-cone points stay visible. A click selects the nearest center within about 10 pixels. No source code or media from her viewer is bundled. FareyVision renderer modules are reused at pinned commit ca809d2; new Lean code and proofs are by this project, not Stange.

The separate Farey L/R demonstration credits Caroline Series (1985). The two lattices have distinct meanings. Research background: Algebraic Number Starscapes — Harriss, Stange and Trettel.
